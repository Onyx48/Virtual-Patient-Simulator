# Five stakeholders want safety, not just realism

VPS Clinical serves five constituencies, and none of them mainly wants "a more realistic chatbot." **Learners want a private, low-stakes rehearsal space before a feared examiner or supervisor watches them, and they will game any score that rewards keyword coverage.** **Educators want fewer SP-logistics headaches and more consistent marking, but only as a formative adjunct they can override.** **Institutional buyers want governance cover, showcase value and evidence of placement readiness. They are slowed by pilotitis, soft-law compliance and a preference for big vendors.** **The vendor's real competitors are in-house university builds and free ChatGPT, not VR incumbents.** **The patients the AI must portray hold TCM and rest-first beliefs, mix languages, defer to clinicians and hide psychological distress behind physical complaints.** The evidence is uneven. Academic literature on AI patients, LLM grading and Singapore governance is reasonably strong. Verbatim learner voice is almost absent: Reddit, The Student Room, HardwareZone, LIHKG and 5ch were all blocked or unindexed, so the only forum read in full was the Japanese board pt-ot-st.net. Most findings came from search snippets, not full texts. Throughout this report, **[E]** marks claims backed by a cited source and **[I]** marks inference that still needs validation. The product implications at the end follow from both, and the inferred ones should be treated as hypotheses to test with SIT, Tung Wah College and hospital partners.

## Learners rehearse for a feared examiner, and will optimise for the checklist

**Daily reality [E].** Placement stress is common and highly variable. It is driven by assessments, workload and patient complexity, and higher stress hurts performance ([CSP/ACPTR](https://acptr.csp.org.uk/node/1321461)). Placement stress was one of the top two reasons students self-referred to UK university wellbeing services ([CSP Frontline](https://www.csp.org.uk/frontline/article/student-placements)). The clinical educator dominates the experience ([Physiother Theory Pract 2022](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/09593985.2022.2069617)). In Japan, **56% of first-year PT/OT students showed state anxiety** ([IJMHP](https://www.techscience.com/IJMHP/v25n7/52894/epub)). Students on short evaluation placements spent about **4 hours a night on notes at home but about 17 minutes a day treating patients** ([J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/cjpt/2002/0/2002_0_809/_article/-char/ja)). At SIT, simulation is officially the "cornerstone" of preparation for the Clinical Practice Examination ([SIT IRR](https://irr.singaporetech.edu.sg/articles/conference_contribution/_From_Classroom_to_Clinic_Enhancing_Physiotherapy_Education_with_Simulation_and_Applied_Learning_Pedagogies/29231681)). SIT has also used senior students as SPs for juniors ([World Physiotherapy](https://www.world.physio/congress-proceeding/developing-junior-physiotherapy-students-musculoskeletal-assessments-senior)).

**How they talk.** The vocabulary is confirmed but the tone is not. "Aggs and eases", the 24-hour pattern, red flags and pain scales show up as rote template and flashcard items ([Physio Newbies](https://thephysionewbies.wordpress.com/2020/11/27/objective-assessment-msk-spine/); [Quizlet](https://quizlet.com/gb/871720171/band-5-physio-interview-questions-flash-cards/)). A UK student describes the OSCE as "you are given a case study, someone else on your course acts as the patient" ([TSR snippet](https://www.thestudentroom.co.uk/showthread.php?t=7079296)). The only verbatim regional voice is Japanese. A supervisor writes that a student who can't keep basic professional manners has "no right" to placement, and lists the top rules as "don't be late; greet energetically". In the same thread, other members push back: "it's normal that students can't do things" ([pt-ot-st.net 4819](https://www.pt-ot-st.net/index.php/bbs/detail/4819)). A student reflects that "what I learned at school often didn't work", and that techniques practised on healthy classmates "don't always work on real patients" ([pt-ot-st.net 5513](https://www.pt-ot-st.net/index.php/bbs/detail/5513)). That theory-practice gap is the clearest learner-voiced need a VPS can answer. **There is no sourced evidence of Singlish, Cantonese-English or Japanese-English phrasing by students [gap].**

**Motivations and fears.** The main drivers are passing the CPE or placement, avoiding judgement, and not falling behind peers. One archived Reddit post captures it: a student "so scared but continued", worried about not graduating with friends ([Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1s4jaek/snapshots/2026-03-27T00%3A11%3A35.174777Z)). After graduation, burnout and the drift from public to private practice dominate. An open letter urges MOH to tackle physio retention ([TOC](https://theonlinecitizen.com/2026/07/17/open-letter-calls-for-straits-times-to-do-a-better-job-at-the-real-issues-with-public-healthcare)), and SIT is surveying burnout in the public sector ([SIT IRR](https://irr.singaporetech.edu.sg/articles/journal_contribution/A_Cross-Sectional_Survey_of_Burnout_Prevalence_Intention_to_Leave_and_Contributing_Stressors_Among_Physiotherapists_in_Singapore_s_Publicly_Funded_Healthcare_Institutions/29410001)). Junior clinicians will therefore be time-poor users who want short sessions with visible gains [I].

**AI patients vs SP actors [E].** Melbourne first-years found an AI patient enjoyable and useful for subjective assessment but weaker for rapport. They named a "digital empathy gap" in text-only interaction ([UniMelb Validitron](https://mdhs.unimelb.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/5426553/MDHS-LT-Conference-Day-1-Validitron-Allison-Ai-Powered-Patient-Sim.pdf)). Acceptance across 1,066 students in five countries was only moderate ([PMC12364448](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12364448/)). With ChatGPT as the SP, **only about 20% wanted AI in future OSCEs** and most preferred a hybrid with human SPs. Inconsistent answers were a key complaint ([medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/highwire/citation/958766/medlars)). One trial found that AI-SP practice raised OSCE scores but not diagnostic accuracy ([chatpaper](https://chatpaper.com/chatpaper/paper/343780); the date needs verifying). That suggests checklist coverage and reasoning are separate outcomes.

**Dark side.** Japanese PT students use ChatGPT heavily. **61.6% of AI users use it weekly for study, but only 15.4% know of any institutional guidance** ([PTR J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ptr/advpub/0/advpub_26-E00045/_article)). Health-profession students are "not universally aware or in agreement" about what counts as AI cheating ([Med Teach 2024](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/0142159X.2024.2385667)). Peer advice to "find the classmates who are really good at it and study with them" points to answers and approaches circulating within cohorts ([Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1tkd7ss/snapshots/2026-05-22T21%3A54%3A29.273415Z)). **No study documents students gaming an LLM patient [gap].** The working risk model [I] is:

- rapid-fire, keyword-stuffed questioning;
- pre-generated ChatGPT question lists on a second screen;
- case details and "answers" shared between cohorts;
- AI-written reflections;
- distrust of any score that looks opaque, especially when ASR mishears accented speech.

## Educators want fewer SP headaches, but insist on staying the judge

**How they talk [E].** The institutional vocabulary is "clinical questioning and reasoning", "history taking", "communication checklist" and "objective feedback". SIT's ALIGN project describes open-ended questioning, an emotional avatar, multiple attempts and end-of-session checklists ([SIT](https://singaporetech.edu.sg/sit-teaching-and-learning-academy/collaborations/t-and-l-showcase/align-salt-projects/training-physiotherapy-students-in-clinical-questioning-and-reasoning-using-ai)). A Minister of State cited SIT's "ClassAId, Virtual Patient System and the Spinal Mobilisation Objective Feedback Tool" ([MOH 2026](https://www.moh.gov.sg/newsroom/speech-by-mdm-rahayu-mahzam--minister-of-state--ministry-of-digital-development-and-information---ministry-of-heatlh-at-the-singapore-international-physiotherapy-congress-2026--11-july-2026--9am/)). When educators talk about validity, they mean agreement with faculty: κ, ICC, and "human oversight".

**Daily reality [E].** SP OSCEs are costly and noisy. Staff variability affects scores as much as student variability does, and it resists rater training. One UK estimate put a 15-station OSCE at about £335 per student ([BMC Med Educ 2024](https://bmcmededuc.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12909-024-05803-6.pdf)). The work starts long before exam day, with scenario writing, SP training and scheduling ([Elevate Health](https://elevatehealth.net/resources/webinars/ssh-virtual-learning-lab-sps-osces/)). Japan has a structural SP shortage: PT schools are limited by SP training costs ([J-STAGE JASEHP](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jasehp/8/0/8_2020-08-11/_article/-char/en)), and Nagasaki launched an AI VP programme because population decline made SPs hard to recruit ([dig.watch](https://dig.watch/updates/nagasaki-university-launches-ai-program-for-medical-student-training)). Placements are scarce, and supervisors are stressed and feel unprepared to evaluate ([PMC5125478](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5125478)). SIT PT requires about 1,000 placement hours ([SIT](https://www.singaporetech.edu.sg/undergraduate-programmes/physiotherapy)).

**Attitude to AI grading [E].** Educators are cautiously positive. Physio educators are generally positive about AI but worry about privacy and bias ([World Physiotherapy](https://world.physio/congress-proceeding/use-artificial-intelligence-physiotherapy-educators-and-researchers)). GPT-4 matched human graders on OSCE communication transcripts at **κ=0.88** ([arXiv 2410.12858](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.12858?context=cs)). Even so, the consistent position is "adjunct with oversight". The Juntendo authors note that text-only scoring misses tone, nonverbal cues and cultural context ([News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20260415/Study-finds-AI-can-match-clinicians-in-interview-assessment.aspx)). A qualitative study judged conversational AI patients fit for supplementary training, not high-stakes assessment ([Frontiers 2025](https://public-pages-files-2025.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2025.1632742/text)). Faculty saw more value in VR simulation than students did, so faculty can be the early adopters ([Simulation & Gaming](https://ideas.repec.org/a/sae/simgam/v53y2022i2p111-134.html)).

**Dark side.** AI feedback errors shape student decisions even after peer discussion ([scoping review, 42 studies](https://tipegitimi.gazi.edu.tr/ai-meded-observatory/articles/applications-and-outcomes-of-large-language-model-generated-feedback-in-undergraduate-medical-education-a-scoping-review)). Between 88% and 94% of faculty worry about cognitive offloading ([EDUCAUSE 2026](https://er.educause.edu/articles/2026/4/listening-to-skepticism-what-faculty-concerns-about-generative-ai-reveal)). The likely failure mode is that overloaded staff rubber-stamp AI scores, and the institution then carries liability for clinically wrong feedback [I]. **Territorial resistance from SP-programme staff, and educators' fear of deskilling or replacement, are undocumented [gap].**

**Culture [E].** In hierarchical, collectivist settings, feedback tends to be one-way and group feedback is preferred ([Soemantri et al. review](https://libmast.utm.my/Record/oai:generic.eprints.org:283911/Description)). Face concerns make anonymous channels useful ([Med Educ, Taiwan](https://bohrium.dp.tech/paper/arxiv/38766732)). Nothing specific to Tung Wah College, Shinshu or NUH was found.

## Buyers purchase governance cover and showcase value, and they stall in pilots

**Procurement [E].** Singapore public-health IT runs through Synapxe, which pilots on hyperscaler stacks such as Google Cloud and Databricks ([IDC Nova](https://www.idcnova.com/html/1/59/153/3345.html); [TechEDT](https://www.techedt.com/synapxe-unveils-ai-partnerships-and-prototypes-to-enhance-singapores-healthcare-system)). Startups are funnelled into grant-funded proof of concept (PoC) and proof of value (PoV). The Startup SG Tech caps are reportedly about S$250k for PoC and S$500k for PoV, but sources conflict ([Wise](https://wise.com/sg/blog/singapore-startup-grants)). "Pilotitis", where many pilots run and few scale, is well documented in health tech generally ([Frontiers 2026](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/digital-health/articles/10.3389/fdgth.2026.1699005/full)). The realistic route in is a department education budget or a cluster-academy pilot. The structural risk is a grant-funded LOI that never converts, because no operating budget exists once the grant ends [I].

**Governance [E].** Singapore governs AI mostly through soft law that compliance teams treat as hard requirements. The PDPC AI advisory guidelines (March 2024) are organised by stage, including procurement ([PDPC](https://pdpc.gov.sg/guidelines-and-consultation/2024/02/advisory-guidelines-on-use-of-personal-data-in-ai-recommendation-and-decision-systems)). **AIHGle 2.0 (10 March 2026) splits accountability between developer, deployer and user** ([MOH](https://moh.gov.sg/licensing-and-regulation/artificial-intelligence-in-healthcare)). Hospital committees may apply it by default even to a training-only tool [I]. Hong Kong's PCPD Model Framework is effectively a buyer's checklist, covering privacy impact assessments, supplier vetting and incident plans ([Freshfields](https://technologyquotient.freshfields.com/post/102jc80/hong-kong-guidance-on-the-procurement-and-use-of-ai-systems)). Japan's MEXT guidance leaves each university to set its own rules ([MEXT](https://www.mext.go.jp/zyoukatsu/ai/moral-ai/assets/pdf/20260324.pdf)), which means per-institution configuration. Separately, SIT reportedly tells students not to use GenAI as a primary source ([AcademicJobs](https://www.academicjobs.com/sg/research-publication-news/singapore-students-views-on-genai-regulations-or-academicjobs-26393)).

**Metrics.** Cost evidence does not reliably favour simulation. In an Australian OT RCT, a simulated placement week cost **AUD$460–1,511 per student versus AUD$144–1,112 for a traditional week**, and **students would pay AUD$533 to keep a real placement** ([Unimelb](https://findanexpert.unimelb.edu.au/scholarlywork/1446891-economic-evaluation-of-simulated-and-traditional-clinical-placements-in-occupational-therapy-education)). SP actors cost roughly US$30–50 an hour according to a vendor source ([Experience Genius](https://www.experiencegeniusacademy.com/blog/creating-a-standardized-patient-program-for-your-school-a-step-by-step-guide)). Institutions headline rollout counts rather than outcomes: SIT's in-house history-taking chatbot reached **170 students**, justified as "repeated practice ... in a safe learning environment" ([SIT/IAL brief](https://www.ial.edu.sg/getattachment/328eb625-bae4-444b-aedb-563aa815b836/Breakout-2a_-ALX2024_SIT_LeeCC_Using-AI-to-develop-conversational-skills_brief.pdf)). Ministerial name-checks and visits such as Seirei's ([Seirei](https://www.seirei.ac.jp/information/20250922-g1e)) show that showcase value is itself a purchase driver. Because SIT already builds its own tools, any external vendor faces a not-invented-here problem with the faculty who own those projects [I]. **No Singapore or Hong Kong SP rates, GeBIZ simulation tenders, simulation-hour accreditation rules, or Tung Wah College AI policy were found [gap].**

## The vendor competes with free ChatGPT and faculty-built prototypes

**Market [E].** Incumbents such as Oxford Medical Simulation and Body Interact sell quote-only, multi-year institutional licences ([Unbound Medicine](https://www.unboundmedicine.com/oadn/view/Virtual%20Simulation%20Reviews/2455046/all/Oxford_Medical_Simulation); [PocketNurse](https://pocketnurse.com/tbr/11-98-7623p-body-interact-for-nursing-education-lite-package-one-year-subscription)). Virti uses a credit and seat model ([SourceForge](https://www.sourceforge.net/software/compare/SimX-vs-Virti)). Physio AI patients are mostly university builds: PolyU, PhysioCheck at the Hospital Authority, Curtin, U-M Flint, OsloMet and Stanford ([PolyU](https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/ai-driven-platform-for-advancing-clinical-reasoning-in-musculoske/); [HA Convention](https://haconvention2026.dryfta.com/abstract-archive/abstract/public/1059/boosting-clinical-decision-making-in-physiotherapy-launch-of-physiocheck-an-effective-ai-assisted-online-simulation-platform); [U-M](https://genai.umich.edu/node/359)). Faculty valued plain ChatGPT as an SP ([medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.03.12.25323842.full.pdf)), which makes free ChatGPT a zero-cost substitute. The defensible value is therefore authoring tools, analytics, validity evidence and governance, not the chatbot itself [I].

**Realities and dark side [E].** The evidence base is small: typical LLM virtual-patient studies involve 10–50 students ([JMIR review 2026](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12811743/)). Overclaiming is common in the sector, for example SimCare's "20 vs 200 encounters" ([HIT Consultant](https://hitconsultant.net/2025/02/27/simcare-ai-raises-2m-for-ai-powered-clinical-training-platform/)). Hallucination rates of 0.31–5% ([JMIR review](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12811743/)) still add up to many wrong patient statements at scale. LLMs also give demographically biased answers in SP roles ([arXiv 2511.02246](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.02246)). ASR is significantly worse on non-native clinical speech ([medRxiv 2025](https://www.medrxiv.org/content/10.1101/2025.08.29.25333548v1.full.txt)) and fails on code-mixed speech ([arXiv 2512.10967](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10967v1)), which is a direct fairness risk for Singlish, Cantonese-accented and Japanese-accented learners. **Grading agreement looks strong in aggregate but is uneven by item.** In one study a GPT-4 SP gave over 99% plausible answers with feedback κ=0.832, yet **8 of 45 categories fell below κ 0.6** ([JMIR Med Educ 2024](https://mededu.jmir.org/2024/1/e59213/PDF)). Judgement-heavy communication items agree worst ([AIME-Con 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.aimecon-wip.31.pdf)). As super admin, the vendor is a PDPA data intermediary ([Pertama Partners](https://www.pertamapartners.com/insights/ai-compliance-education-sector-regulatory)). It must disclose LLM sub-processors such as OpenRouter and say whether they train on student data. It also faces unbilled custom case work at each institution [I]. **No figures on sales-cycle length, cost per session, or vendor breaches were found [gap].**

## Patients the AI must portray mix TCM, deference and somatic language

**Speech and beliefs [E].** In Singapore and Hong Kong, many patients use TCM alongside physiotherapy. In SGH's 204-patient trial, tuina matched physio on pain at 2 months, and physio did better on function at 5 months. A TCM practitioner in the same report says "the tendency is that you will have to come back to me", while an SGH physio says the aim is "to get them to be responsible for their treatment" ([SGH](https://www.sgh.com.sg/news/patient-care/tcm-tuina-therapy-as-effective-as-physiotherapy-for-short-term-relief-of-lower-back-pain-sgh-study)). Hong Kong patients see bone-setters (跌打) for MSK problems ([CUHK Varsity](https://varsity.com.cuhk.edu.hk/varsity/0011/pf_tida/tida.htm)) and code-mix by embedding English terms in Cantonese ([PolyU thesis](https://theses.lib.polyu.edu.hk/handle/200/3446)). In a reported Pfizer survey, **80% of Japanese chronic-pain patients use ideophones** such as zuki-zuki and piri-piri ([UniSZA record](https://cendekia.unisza.edu.my/neuaxis-e/Record/curtin-20.500.11937-46639/Description)). Elderly Singaporeans face language barriers and rely on relatives to interpret ([NTU chapter](https://www.taylorfrancis.com/chapters/oa-edit/10.4324/9781003125815-11/languages-gone-aging-changing-multilingual-linguistic-ecology-ng-bee-chin-francesco-cavallaro)). Singaporean and Taiwanese Chinese held more fear-avoidant beliefs about back pain than Australians (pre-2015, students rather than patients; [ECU](https://ro.ecu.edu.au/ecuworks/549)). Hong Kong fracture patients avoided painkillers for fear of side effects ([PolyU](https://research.polyu.edu.hk/en/publications/the-pain-experience-and-beliefs-of-chinese-patients-who-have-sust-2/)).

**Sensitive disclosure [E].** Stigma and face push patients to report fatigue and poor sleep rather than mood ([Springer 2023](https://link.springer.com/10.1007/s40120-023-00471-y); [eCALD](https://resources.ecald.com/working-with-asian-mental-health-clients-supplementary-resource/section-ii/cultural-issues-to-be-aware-of-when-working-with-asian-clients-in-general)). The somatisation claim itself is contested. Asian families are reluctant to discuss sexual health in Singapore ([SMA News](https://sma.org.sg/UploadedImg/1761733023_2025OctFeature.pdf)). **Nothing was found on how Singaporean, Hong Kong or Japanese patients answer bowel or bladder (cauda equina) screening, on Japanese gaman/enryo in physio, or on Singlish pain vocabulary such as "heaty" or "angin" [gap].** The SP craft literature is solid. SPs should be "realistic and repeatable" ([ASPE standards](https://link.springer.com/10.1186/s41077-017-0043-4)). NBME codes each SP response as Correct, Too Much Information, Too Little Information, Incorrect or Not Applicable ([Emerson et al.](https://aclanthology.org/2025.aimecon-main.25.pdf)). SP feedback forms flag the "over-enthusiastic" SP who volunteers unasked information ([AKU form](https://www.aku.edu:443/mcpk/ded/exam-cell/Documents/SPs%20Feedback%20Form.pdf)). Portrayals hold together better when the SP has a rich backstory ([AJHPE](https://www.ajol.info/index.php/ajhpe/article/view/104894)).

**How history-taking OSCEs really go.** The evidence supports this picture: a peer or SP plays the patient, scoring follows an (often electronic) checklist ([TDMU](https://www.tdmu.edu.ua/en/?p=186229)), students find it stressful ([J Mod Rehabil](https://doaj.org/article/79f863d2445e41419224498e9f93bd20)), and examiner variance is large. Japan formalised Pre-OSCE and Post-OSCE stages with a 2025 manual ([pt-ot-st.net](https://www.pt-ot-st.net/index.php/topics/detail/1724)). Two things are inference only [I]: that students run memorised SIN, aggs/eases and red-flag scripts, and that SPs leak information when they overact. **Forum testimony on this was blocked.**

## Product implications

The table below separates evidence-backed changes from inferred ones.

| Area | Implication | Basis |
|---|---|---|
| Virtual patient | Keep a locked fact sheet per case and answer only what is asked. Self-score each patient turn with the NBME five categories (Correct, Too Much Information, Too Little Information, Incorrect, Not Applicable) to catch both inconsistency and over-sharing. | [E] inconsistency is the top AI-SP complaint; ASPE/NBME |
| Virtual patient | Give each case a deep backstory: job, family, TCM or bone-setter history that is disclosed only when asked, rest-first and imaging expectations, painkiller avoidance, and a family interpreter for elderly personas. | [E] SGH/HK/NTU; persona detail [I] |
| Virtual patient | Use culture packs for code-mixing: Cantonese with embedded English for Hong Kong, ideophones for Japan, Singlish particles for Singapore. Answer mood questions somatically at first, and reveal psychosocial and continence information only after rapport or a normalising question. Let elderly personas give polite "yes" answers to closed or jargon-heavy questions. | [E] code-mixing, ideophones, face; Singlish and continence behaviour [I] |
| Virtual patient | Use voice and an emotional avatar to narrow the empathy gap, but keep text as a fallback. | [E] Melbourne; SIT ALIGN |
| Feedback/grading | Label all scoring "formative". Educators sign off before any score counts, can override it, and every change is logged. | [E] adjunct consensus; [I] rubber-stamping and liability |
| Feedback/grading | Score question quality, sequencing, open versus closed questions, and reasoning, not just checklist coverage. Penalise rapid-fire keyword stuffing. Randomise case details per attempt to blunt cohort answer-sharing and ChatGPT pre-scripting. | [E] OSCE-up/diagnosis-flat; [I] gaming model |
| Feedback/grading | Show students the transcript, let them flag ASR errors before a grade stands, and audit word error rate by accent group. | [E] ASR bias |
| Feedback/grading | Publish agreement with faculty per rubric item, not a pooled κ. Hold empathy and nonverbal items to human moderation. | [E] 8 of 45 categories below κ 0.6 |
| Feedback/grading | Check reflections for AI-generated text, and make integrity rules explicit to students. | [E] 15.4% aware of guidance |
| Dashboards (educator) | Show cohort-level patterns, such as "62% skipped the cauda equina screen", rather than individual leaderboards. Include a calibration view comparing AI and faculty scores, and an override queue. | [E] group-feedback norms; teachers value pattern-spotting |
| Dashboards (student) | Keep progress private and attempt-based, so students can fail without losing face, and link it to CPE and placement readiness. | [E] stress; [I] face |
| Dashboards (admin/buyer) | Provide accreditation-ready exports: attempts, red-flag coverage, readiness trends. Include a governance pack (PDPA data-flow map, pre-filled PCPD privacy impact assessment, LLM sub-processor and no-training disclosure, Singapore data residency, incident plan) and showcase-friendly adoption metrics. | [E] PDPC/PCPD/AIHGle; [I] buyer asks |
| Super admin | Track per-institution configuration (MEXT rules differ by university), custom-case effort, LLM cost per session, and hallucination flags. Set pilot conversion criteria and a budget owner at contract signing. | [E] pilotitis; [I] cost tracking |

## Conclusion

Every stakeholder shares one underlying need: **psychological and institutional safety.** Students want to fail in private, educators want to keep the final judgement, buyers want governance cover, and the patients being portrayed hide distress to save face. Realism is necessary but not sufficient. VPS wins by being the place where history-taking can go wrong safely and leave an audit trail, not by being the most lifelike chatbot. The largest strategic exposure is grading fairness: ASR errors on accented speech, gameable checklists, and AI scores treated as final. One mis-scored Singlish speaker or one rubber-stamped wrong feedback could undo educator trust faster than any feature builds it.

The most urgent research to close the gaps is a short manual, logged-in pass over r/physiotherapy, r/PTschool, r/SGexams, HardwareZone, LIHKG and 5ch, plus five to ten interviews each with SIT and Tung Wah College students and educators. The interviews should cover four things: phrasing and code-switching, gaming behaviour, how comfortable students are with continence questions, and trust in AI scores. Until that is done, the persona speech patterns and the gaming model above remain hypotheses, not findings.
