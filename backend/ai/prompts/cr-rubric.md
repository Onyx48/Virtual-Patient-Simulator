5. Clinical Reasoning assessment rubric
Purpose: assess a learner's ability to interpret and synthesise the clinical information they gathered in history taking and physical observation, and to reach a justified, prioritised and safe working diagnosis.
The performance levels are the same as in the History Taking and Treatment Plan rubrics, so all competencies use one scale on the learner dashboard.

Criterion 1: Information Gathering and Cue Recognition (20%)
Finding the information is assessed in history taking; this criterion assesses recognising which findings are relevant.
Level
 | Descriptor
 | 
4
 | Consistently identifies all relevant subjective and objective findings. Recognises significant biological, psychological and social factors. Gathers information efficiently and purposefully.
 | 
3
 | Identifies most relevant findings and major contributing factors. Minor omissions do not significantly affect reasoning.
 | 
2
 | Identifies some relevant findings but overlooks important information or contributing factors. Data collection lacks focus.
 | 
1
 | Misses key information and demonstrates limited understanding of important patient factors.
 | 
Criterion 2: Hypothesis Generation and Prioritisation (25%)
Level
 | Descriptor
 | 
4
 | Develops logical, comprehensive and prioritised hypotheses. Generates at least two hypotheses. Demonstrates consideration of differential diagnoses and biopsychosocial contributors.
 | 
3
 | Generates appropriate primary hypotheses with reasonable justification. Considers alternative explanations.
 | 
2
 | Generates limited hypotheses with incomplete justification. Prioritisation is unclear or inconsistent.
 | 
1
 | Hypotheses are inaccurate, poorly justified or absent.
 | 
Criterion 3: Analysis and Interpretation of Findings (25%)
"Examination findings" refers to the learner's own physical observation of the patient's movements.
Level
 | Descriptor
 | 
4
 | Integrates information from multiple sources to form a coherent explanation of the patient's presentation. Accurately interprets examination findings and identifies relationships between impairments, activity limitations and participation restrictions.
 | 
3
 | Correctly interprets most findings and demonstrates reasonable integration of information.
 | 
2
 | Interpretation is partially accurate but lacks synthesis across different information sources.
 | 
1
 | Findings are misinterpreted or discussed in isolation without meaningful integration.
 | 
Criterion 4: Red Flags and Safety Screening (15%)
Applies to every case. Where a case has no red flags, the learner is assessed on justifying why it is safe to proceed.
Level
 | Descriptor
 | 
4
 | Systematically screens for red flags, precautions and contraindications relevant to this patient's history and physical observation. Correctly identifies their presence or absence and justifies this from the findings, explains their significance, and makes an appropriate decision on referral, onward triage or proceeding safely.
 | 
3
 | Identifies the main red flags and precautions relevant to the presentation, or reasonably justifies their absence, and makes an appropriate decision on referral or proceeding. Minor omissions do not compromise patient safety.
 | 
2
 | Considers red flags in a limited or generic way, without linking them to this patient, or states that there are none without justification. Some relevant flags or precautions are missed, or the referral decision is unclear.
 | 
1
 | Does not consider red flags, or misses or misinterprets findings that require urgent referral.
 | 
Criterion 5: Justification and Reflection (15%)
Assessed through the rationale the learner gives throughout the session, and their plan for the physical examination: whether it follows logically from their hypotheses and would help confirm or rule them out.
Level
 | Descriptor
 | 
4
 | Clearly articulates rationale for decisions using clinical evidence, patient factors and professional judgement. Demonstrates insightful reflection and identifies future learning needs.
 | 
3
 | Provides reasonable justification with some reference to evidence and patient factors. Reflection demonstrates learning.
 | 
2
 | Justification is superficial or inconsistently supported. Reflection is descriptive rather than analytical.
 | 
1
 | Limited or absent justification. Reflection lacks insight.
 | 
Global rating of clinical reasoning
The independence cap is applied to each criterion before the overall grade is calculated.
Grade
 | Descriptor
 | 
A (85-100%)
 | Demonstrates advanced clinical reasoning with independent synthesis, strong justification and patient-centred decision-making.
 | 
B (70-84%)
 | Demonstrates sound clinical reasoning with minor omissions or inconsistencies.
 | 
C (55-69%)
 | Demonstrates basic but adequate reasoning. Requires prompting to connect findings and justify decisions.
 | 
D (40-54%)
 | Demonstrates fragmented reasoning with significant guidance required.
 | 
F (<40%)
 | Unable to integrate information effectively or formulate appropriate clinical decisions.
