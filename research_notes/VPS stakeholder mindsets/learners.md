# Learner Mindsets: PT/OT Students and Junior Clinicians (SG/SIT, Japan, HK) as VPS Clinical Users

Method note: about 14 tool calls. Most evidence comes from search-result abstracts and snippets. Full-text fetches of the key sources (Melbourne Validitron PDF, SIT burnout survey) were blocked (HTTP 403), so the figures below are as reported in abstracts. I did not retrieve Reddit threads directly. Forum-quote evidence is therefore thin and is marked as such.

## 1. How learners talk (jargon, Singlish, tone)

### Takeaway
I found no sourced corpus of how PT/OT students actually phrase things (HPC, aggs/eases, SIN, red flags, P&N) or of Singlish in clinical talk. The only usable evidence is indirect: placement guidance treats the "subjective assessment" as the core skill learners are judged on, and Asian students are described as less assertive, with language a major barrier.

### Cited Findings
- Placement guidance names "nailing your subjective assessment" as a milestone worth acknowledging. It warns that rushing to treatment before completing the subjective and objective assessment is the most common mistake and the one most likely to concern educators — [HealthTimes, real advice from physio grads](https://healthtimes.com.au/hub/physiotherapy/8/news/nc1/real-advice-from-physio-grads/9580); [Physio placement survival guide](https://physiohub-8bvswwzv.manus.space/blog/physiotherapy-clinical-placement-survival-guide)
- In the East, doctors tend to be perceived within a hierarchy, and Asian students are often seen as less assertive. The authors describe that view as Western-centred. Linguistic issues were the major barrier to learning communication skills — [IJME, pre-tertiary education and attitudes to communication skills](https://www.ijme.net/archive/10/pre-tertiary-education-and-attitudes-towards-communication-skills/xml)
- For foreign students in China, the local language spoken by patients was the most common difficulty in history taking — [BMC Med Educ 2019, PMC6466762](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC6466762/)

### Inferences
- Learners are likely to use shorthand (aggs/eases, 24-hr pattern, red-flag screens) in notes and with tutors, and plainer or code-switched language with patients. The VPS speech pipeline should be tested on clinical abbreviations spoken aloud and on Singlish particles ("lah", "ah", "can or not"). This is not sourced; it is a test plan to validate.

### Gaps
- No real forum quotes on jargon or Singlish were retrieved. The next step is targeted searches of r/physiotherapy, r/PTschool, r/SGexams, HardwareZone and Japanese 2ch/5ch PT boards.

## 2. Daily reality: workload, OSCE and placement stress, hierarchy

### Takeaway
Placement stress is common and varies a lot between students. It is driven by assessment, workload and patient complexity, and the clinical educator dominates the experience. At SIT, simulation is already the "cornerstone" of preparing students for the Clinical Practice Examination (CPE).

### Cited Findings
- UK cohort (109 students, 159 placements): stress and anxiety were highly variable and higher levels hurt academic performance. Drivers were patient complexity, assessments, workload, poor health and family issues — [CSP/ACPTR cohort study](https://acptr.csp.org.uk/node/1321461)
- Placement-related stress was one of the top two reasons students self-referred to university wellbeing services for anxiety — [CSP Frontline, student placements](https://www.csp.org.uk/frontline/article/student-placements)
- The clinical educator is pivotal: students use talking to the educator or ward staff as a coping strategy, and this varies by year of study — [Physiother Theory Pract 2022](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/09593985.2022.2069617)
- Peer-assisted learning reduced anxiety and increased felt safety, but was not seen as a replacement for educator observation — [IJPBLHSC](https://publications.coventry.ac.uk/index.php/pblh/article/download/654/832)
- Archived Reddit post: a student who was frightened after a preceptor raised a problem, "so scared but continued," struggled to finish assessments on time and worried about not graduating with their friends — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1s4jaek/snapshots/2026-03-27T00%3A11%3A35.174777Z)
- SIT: simulation-based education "forms the cornerstone" of the programme's instructional design and prepares students for the CPE through realistic scenarios and reflection — [SIT IRR conference contribution](https://irr.singaporetech.edu.sg/articles/conference_contribution/_From_Classroom_to_Clinic_Enhancing_Physiotherapy_Education_with_Simulation_and_Applied_Learning_Pedagogies/29231681). SIT staff are studying how the simulation module PTY3016 affects CPE readiness and outcomes — [SIT directory, Brenda Ong](https://www.singaporetech.edu.sg/directory/professional-officers/brenda-ong-geok-ting)
- SIT has used senior students as standardised patients for junior musculoskeletal assessments, and moved this to telehealth during COVID — [WCPT congress proceeding](https://www.world.physio/congress-proceeding/developing-junior-physiotherapy-students-musculoskeletal-assessments-senior)
- Japan (Fujita Health University): 56% of first-year PT/OT students had state anxiety and 52% had trait anxiety. Anxiety was lower at first clinical contact than at enrolment — [IJMHP, techscience](https://www.techscience.com/IJMHP/v25n7/52894/epub)
- Singapore novice physiotherapists' perceived challenges in clinical practice have been studied. I could not access the content — [record](https://library.kab.ac.ug/Record/doaj-art-a759c65a649f4a13bc6bcd6c08659d6a)

### Inferences
- The VPS is likely to be used most heavily in the weeks before the CPE and placements, as a low-stakes rehearsal. Positioning it as "practice before the educator watches you" fits the stress profile.

### Gaps
- I found no first-hand accounts of history-taking OSCEs being run on memorised scripts or checklist ticking among SG/JP/HK PT students.

## 3. Motivations and fears (failing, judgement, burnout, pay)

### Takeaway
Fear of failing placements and being judged by educators is well documented. Singapore physio careers show public-to-private attrition and burnout concerns. Pay data is inconsistent.

### Cited Findings
- A July 2026 open letter says physiotherapists are moving from public healthcare to private practice and urges MOH to tackle burnout and retention rather than only recruitment — [The Online Citizen](https://theonlinecitizen.com/2026/07/17/open-letter-calls-for-straits-times-to-do-a-better-job-at-the-real-issues-with-public-healthcare)
- An SIT cross-sectional survey covers burnout prevalence, intention to leave and stressors among Singapore public-sector physiotherapists. Only the aims were visible, and the listing notes that local data are limited — [SIT IRR](https://irr.singaporetech.edu.sg/articles/journal_contribution/A_Cross-Sectional_Survey_of_Burnout_Prevalence_Intention_to_Leave_and_Contributing_Stressors_Among_Physiotherapists_in_Singapore_s_Publicly_Funded_Healthcare_Institutions/29410001)
- Singapore pay (sources conflict):
  - Payscale reports an average base of about S$49k a year — [Payscale](https://www.payscale.com/research/SG/Job=Physiotherapist/Salary)
  - Payscale reports about S$100k a year late-career — [Payscale late career](https://www.payscale.com/research/SG/Job=Physiotherapist/Salary/8b21faab/Late-Career-Singapore)
  - Paylab puts the middle 80% at S$2,768–7,771 a month — [Paylab](https://paylab.com/sg/salaryinfo/medicine-social-care/physiotherapist)
- Comparators: 49% of surveyed Scottish physios are considering leaving — [STV](https://news.stv.tv/scotland/physiotherapist-staff-shortages-taking-a-devastating-toll-on-nhs-patients). In New Zealand, 39.6% intend to leave their job and 19.4% the profession — [AcademicJobs, Otago study](https://www.academicjobs.com/nz/research-publication-news/40percent-public-physiotherapists-want-to-quit-otago-study-or-academicjobs-nz-12262)

### Inferences
- Junior clinicians who are time-poor and at risk of burnout will want short sessions that show visible competency gains. Students will be motivated mainly by passing the CPE or placements.

### Gaps
- No Singapore burnout prevalence figure could be extracted. No HK or Japan pay or attrition data was found.

## 4. Attitudes to AI patients vs SP actors

### Takeaway
Students generally find AI patients useful and enjoyable for practising subjective history taking and repetition. They find them weaker for rapport and empathy (a "digital empathy gap"), report inconsistency and technical glitches, and mostly prefer a hybrid with human SPs. Acceptance of AI chatbots among physio students is moderate.

### Cited Findings
- Melbourne (first-year physio, 88 of 135 responded): the AI patient was enjoyable and useful for subjective assessment, less useful for communication and rapport. Students named a "digital empathy gap" in text-only interaction, and supervisor feedback was mixed — [UniMelb Validitron "Allison" slides](https://mdhs.unimelb.edu.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0003/5426553/MDHS-LT-Conference-Day-1-Validitron-Allison-Ai-Powered-Patient-Sim.pdf)
- Valencia (156 first-year physio students): students who built and interviewed patient bots rated them fairly highly for interview skills and comfort with patients. This is a conference paper — [IATED 2025](https://library.iated.org/view/ESCRICHEESCUDER2025USE)
- International study (1,066 students, 5 countries): moderate acceptance of AI chatbots by physio students under the Technology Acceptance Model — [PMC12364448](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC12364448/)
- Italy (589 students): 95.3% had heard of chatbots, but 53.7% had never used them for academic work — [BMC Med Educ 2025](https://biomedcentral-bmcmededuc.publicaciones.saludcastillayleon.es/articles/10.1186/s12909-025-07176-w); [Future Reps summary](https://futurereps.beehiiv.com/p/the-new-study-partner-ai-in-physical-therapy-education)
- ChatGPT as SP: participants valued its responsiveness but reported technical problems and inconsistent answers. Only about 20% wanted AI in future OSCEs, and many preferred a hybrid with human SPs — [medRxiv](https://www.medrxiv.org/highwire/citation/958766/medlars)
- AIPatient (LLM SP) was compared with human SPs on OSCE checklist items elicited, and rated on coherence, relevance and emotional realism — [arXiv 2409.18924](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.18924)
- In a multi-agent AI-SP randomised trial (N=100), OSCE scores improved but diagnostic accuracy did not. The listing date looks wrong and should be verified — [chatpaper](https://chatpaper.com/chatpaper/paper/343780)
- Japan: VR facility tours as a substitute for early-exposure placements gave high satisfaction, but some students had eye fatigue or motion sickness — [J-STAGE JOTR 2023](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/jotr/42/2/42_236/_article/-char/en)
- US OT students: a VR clinical-exam simulation reduced state anxiety in a small, non-randomised trial — [JMIR Serious Games 2025 (DOAJ)](https://doaj.org/article/00d17bb4aa024232bdc13951dadedbb8)
- Visiting Seirei Christopher University (Japan) students observed AI-based simulation learning at SIT in September 2025 — [Seirei](https://www.seirei.ac.jp/information/20250922-g1e)

### Inferences
- Voice plus an avatar (VPS) directly targets the text-only "empathy gap." Inconsistency, where the patient changes facts between turns, is the most likely credibility killer.
- The OSCE-up, diagnosis-flat result suggests AI-SP practice can train checklist coverage without improving clinical reasoning. This supports VPS's separate clinical-reasoning stage.

### Gaps
- No Hong Kong-specific study of AI patients was found, and no SIT learner-perception data on AI simulation.

## 5. Dark side: gaming, integrity, distrust of AI scores

### Takeaway
I found no direct study of students gaming an LLM patient or grader. The adjacent evidence is that health-profession students already use generative AI without a shared view of what counts as cheating.

### Cited Findings
- Survey of about 400 students across 12 health programmes (Medical Teacher 2024): students use generative AI but are "not universally aware or in agreement" about how it threatens integrity. The authors call for explicit faculty guidance — [Kazley et al., Med Teach 2024](https://informahealthcare.com/doi/full/10.1080/0142159X.2024.2385667)
- DPT students: 78.9% (cohort 1) and 74.4% (cohort 2) considered AI/ChatGPT use on essays to be cheating — [IJAHSP](https://nsuworks.nova.edu/ijahsp/vol24/iss2/25)
- Medical students mostly use ChatGPT to understand concepts and prepare for exams. The authors raise concerns about information verification and integrity — [JMIR Formative 2026](https://formative.jmir.org/2026/1/e76838/PDF)
- In Egypt, 44.5% of physio students use AI several times a week and 18.1% daily — [ISCSR conference](https://iscsr3.conferences.ekb.eg/paper?manu=11720)
- Peer advice on Reddit: "find the classmates who are really good at it and study with them." This suggests answers and approaches circulate within cohorts — [Reddit snapshot](https://reddit.sentinel-team.org/posts/1tkd7ss/snapshots/2026-05-22T21%3A54%3A29.273415Z)

### Inferences (unsourced risk model for design)
- Checklist-scored history taking invites rapid-fire, keyword-stuffed questioning. Running ChatGPT on a second screen is a realistic risk, as is passing scenarios and answers between cohorts. Mitigations to consider: score question quality and sequencing as well as coverage, randomise case details, and check reflections for AI-generated text. Any score students see as opaque is likely to be distrusted, as the mixed supervisor feedback in Melbourne hints.

### Gaps
- No empirical data was found on gaming AI virtual patients, on trust in AI rubric scores in PT/OT, or on scenario sharing at SIT, HK or Japanese schools.

## 6. Cultural: hierarchy, face, sensitive questions, language and ASR

### Takeaway
Hierarchy, lower assertiveness and language barriers are documented in Asian clinical communication training. Reluctance to discuss sexual and sensitive topics is noted in Singapore. Speech recognition is measurably worse for non-native and code-mixed English, which is a direct risk for voice-based VPS with Singaporean, HK and Japanese learners.

### Cited Findings
- East Asian clinicians tend to be perceived within a hierarchy, Asian students are seen as less assertive, and language is the major barrier to learning communication skills — [IJME](https://www.ijme.net/archive/10/pre-tertiary-education-and-attitudes-towards-communication-skills/xml)
- Singapore: Asian parents are reluctant to discuss sexual health because of traditional culture and religion — [SMA News Oct 2025](https://sma.org.sg/UploadedImg/1761733023_2025OctFeature.pdf)
- Malaysian final-year medical students' attitudes to sexual history taking and to the training they receive have been studied (details not retrieved) — [search result cluster; BMC Res Notes, via UiTM](https://update.library.uitm.edu.my/Record/2-s2.0-84931078936/Details)
- Whisper and WhisperX had significantly higher error rates on non-native English clinical speech, and GPT-4o post-correction recovered much of the loss — [medRxiv 2025](https://www.medrxiv.org/highwire/citation/1057449/refworks-tagged)
- Medical ASR performs reasonably on Indian English but fails on code-mixed or vernacular speech — [arXiv 2512.10967](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.10967v1)
- ASR errors concentrate on medical entities (drug names, diagnoses) even when overall word error rate is low. Fine-tuning cut medical word error rate by about 25–34% — [chatpaper, AfriSpeech clinical](https://chatpaper.com/paper/30883)
- Medical ASR accuracy shows large regional gaps (Nigeria, UK, US) — [arXiv 2501.15310](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.15310v1)

### Inferences
- Singlish code-mixing and Japanese- or Cantonese-accented English are likely to raise misrecognition. When a student is wrongly penalised for an ASR error, they will blame the grader, which fuels distrust. Showing the transcript and letting students flag errors is advisable.
- Face concerns may make students skip bowel/bladder (cauda equina) screening questions with a human SP. An AI patient may be a psychologically safer place to rehearse them, though this is an untested hypothesis.

### Gaps
- No study was found on PT/OT students' reluctance to ask bowel/bladder questions in Asia, or on Mandarin, Japanese or Cantonese language choice in simulation.
