**Access note (important):** Direct fetches of reddit.com and old.reddit.com were refused ("unable to fetch"). Fetches through Reddit mirror front-ends (redlib) were blocked by the tool's safety classifier. The Student Room returned HTTP 403. The search tool hardly indexes Reddit, HardwareZone, LIHKG, 5ch, Yahoo Chiebukuro or note.com, so no threads from those sites turned up. The only forum I could read in full was the Japanese PT/OT/ST board pt-ot-st.net. **This file therefore contains very few verbatim learner quotes.** The English-language community voice is mostly a gap and needs a manual pass (a logged-in browser, or Reddit's API with credentials).

## 1. History-taking OSCE reality (checklists, SIN, aggs/eases, red flags, nerves, SPs)

### Takeaway
I could not retrieve any forum quotes on OSCE scripts, SIN factors, aggs/eases or strange SP actors. What I did find confirms the vocabulary and the format: a peer or SP plays the patient, scoring is by checklist, and students find it stressful.

### Cited Findings
- UK physio student describing the OSCE format: "you are given a case study, someone else on your course acts as the patient" (search snippet only; the page returned 403) — [The Student Room](https://www.thestudentroom.co.uk/showthread.php?t=7079296)
- A student physio blog defines "aggs and eases" as the factors that worsen or ease symptoms, and lists them with the 24-hour pattern in the assessment template — [The Physio Newbies](https://thephysionewbies.wordpress.com/2020/11/27/objective-assessment-msk-spine/)
- A Band 5 interview flashcard deck lists "Aggs and Eases", red flags and pain scales as standard rote items. This is evidence that the material is memorised as a checklist — [Quizlet](https://quizlet.com/gb/871720171/band-5-physio-interview-questions-flash-cards/)
- Academic: physio students rated the OSCE stressful, and authors recommended more time per station and practice sessions — [J Mod Rehabil 2023](https://doaj.org/article/79f863d2445e41419224498e9f93bd20)
- Stations are scored on (electronic) checklists — [TDMU](https://www.tdmu.edu.ua/en/?p=186229)
- Japan: the JPTA describes OSCE as a skills exam with simulated patients, split into Pre-OSCE and Post-OSCE. An OSCE manual (1st ed.) was published in 2025 — [pt-ot-st.net topic](https://www.pt-ot-st.net/index.php/topics/detail/1724)

### Inferences
- The jargon a VPS should recognise and expect (aggs/eases, 24-hr pattern, red flags, subjective/objective) is confirmed as checklist-driven. Students probably run through it as a script, which is a likely route for gaming an AI grader that rewards keyword coverage.

### Gaps
- No verbatim quotes on "SIN factors", memorised scripts, examiner behaviour or SPs overacting (Reddit and TSR were blocked).

## 2. Placement / clinical educator horror stories, hierarchy, burnout, pay, "just exercises" cynicism

### Takeaway
The only verbatim material is Japanese, and it shows a strict, etiquette-heavy supervisor culture. English-language sources confirm new-grad burnout only through articles and studies, not forum quotes.

### Cited Findings
- JP supervisor: 「まず社会人としての常識が守れないようでしたらそもそも臨床実習を受ける権利はありません。」("if you can't keep basic professional manners you have no right to placement") — [pt-ot-st.net, Aug 2022](https://www.pt-ot-st.net/index.php/bbs/detail/4819)
- JP supervisor's top rules: 「１．遅刻しないこと。」「２．あいさつを元気よく行うこと。」("don't be late; greet energetically") — [pt-ot-st.net, Aug 2022](https://www.pt-ot-st.net/index.php/bbs/detail/4819)
- Counter-voice from the same thread: 「この程度で叱るというようなバイザーは不適格だと私は考えています。」("a supervisor who scolds over this is unfit") — [pt-ot-st.net](https://www.pt-ot-st.net/index.php/bbs/detail/4819)
- 「学生はできなくなくて当たり前です。」("it's normal that students can't do things") — [pt-ot-st.net](https://www.pt-ot-st.net/index.php/bbs/detail/4819)
- Search summary of a different pt-ot-st.net thread (not verified verbatim): a supervisor recalls that in their own training days students were expected to stay up all night — [pt-ot-st.net bbs 4768](https://www.pt-ot-st.net/index.php/bbs/detail/4768)
- JP student on the theory–practice gap: 「学校で学んできたことが、なかなか通用しなかったことが印象的でした。」("what I learned at school often didn't work") — [pt-ot-st.net, Sep 2023](https://www.pt-ot-st.net/index.php/bbs/detail/5513)
- Assessments practised on healthy classmates 「必ずしも様々な患者さんには通用しない」("don't always work on real patients") — [pt-ot-st.net](https://www.pt-ot-st.net/index.php/bbs/detail/5513)
- A student apologising to a patient, 「すみません、時間がかかって。。。。」, and the patient's reply, 「大丈夫、どうせ暇だし（笑」("fine, I'm bored anyway lol") — [pt-ot-st.net](https://www.pt-ot-st.net/index.php/bbs/detail/5513)
- 「当時はレポート提出は大変でしたが」("report submissions were brutal back then") — [pt-ot-st.net](https://www.pt-ot-st.net/index.php/bbs/detail/5513)
- JP survey: on short evaluation placements, students averaged about 4 hours a night on notes and assignments at home, against about 17 minutes a day of treatment and about 340 minutes of observation (search summary of an academic source) — [J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/cjpt/2002/0/2002_0_809/_article/-char/ja)
- Australian new-grad physios are "highly engaged and satisfied but edging toward burnout" (study title) — [ACU research bank](https://acuresearchbank.acu.edu.au/item/8z63q/the-first-year-of-private-practice-new-graduate-physiotherapists-are-highly-engaged-and-satisfied-but-edging-toward-burnout); [Australian Physio](https://australian.physio/node/4195)
- A podcast episode titled "Burned out in 3 years" shows that burnout is a mainstream PT topic — [GBU podcast](https://www.buzzsprout.com/2377028/episodes/17748788-gbu-s2-e11-burned-out-in-3-years?t=0)

### Inferences
- In Japan, placement stress centres on etiquette, report volume and a feared バイザー (supervisor), so students may find a low-stakes practice patient attractive.
- The theory–practice gap students describe (healthy classmates vs real patients) is exactly the gap a VPS claims to fill. It is a strong theme for messaging.

### Gaps
- No verbatim Reddit/US/UK/AU quotes on CI horror stories, pay ("PT pay is a joke"-type) or the "just giving exercises" cynicism. Those sites could not be fetched.

## 3. AI/ChatGPT for study, AI patients, AI grading, cheating, sharing past OSCE cases

### Takeaway
Survey data shows very high ChatGPT use among PT students and little awareness of institutional guidance. DPT students themselves frame exam and writing misuse as cheating. I retrieved no forum quotes.

### Cited Findings
- Japanese PT students (4 programs): 93.1% of AI users used free ChatGPT, 61.6% used it weekly for PT study, and only 15.4% knew of official institutional guidance — [Physical Therapy Research (J-STAGE)](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/ptr/advpub/0/advpub_26-E00045/_article)
- DPT students viewed ChatGPT on exams and writing as cheating or plagiarism, and worried about the effect on clinical practice — [IJAHSP](https://nsuworks.nova.edu/ijahsp/vol24/iss2/25)
- Health-professions students' views on whether ChatGPT is cheating; the authors call for specific faculty guidance (Medical Teacher 2024) — [PubMed mirror](https://api-pubmed-ncbi.laibokeji.com/39099009)

### Inferences
- High use plus little guidance suggests students will use ChatGPT freely to prep for or "solve" AI-patient cases. Graders should assume students have pre-generated question lists.

### Gaps
- No forum material on AI patients, AI grading or sharing past-year OSCE cases.
